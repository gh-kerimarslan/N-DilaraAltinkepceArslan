Budun

Edirne Biennial • Current Edition
"The concept of 'unification' has always held a deep fascination for me. Beyond its root of becoming 'one', I perceive it not as singularization, but as an endless multiplication. Every breathing and non-breathing element of nature fulfills this word for me, leaving little room for alternative meanings.

Every image that catches our eye the moment we open them is a form of unification. Even with the same eye looking at the same object, the state of being is forever altered. Thus, unifications are perpetually new, filled with discovery. They allow humans to truly feel alive—or rather, to sense the essence of living.

Many of us attempt to comprehend life, while some dedicate their entire existences to researching and writing about it. But do we ever try to simply feel life? If we did not forget that, despite our ability to speak and reason, we remain animals at our core, would we yield more space to our instincts? Are our feelings paying the toll for these two intellectual privileges?"
