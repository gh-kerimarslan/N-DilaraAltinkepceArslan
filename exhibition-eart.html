Group Exhibition • EArt Gallery • 2025

Today
(Bugün)

140 x 140 cm | Thread on raw cotton fabric

In Central Asia, within the framework of the Tengrism belief, the universe is perceived in three realms: sky, earth-water, and the underworld. This tripartite order forms the foundation of the transitions established by shamans (kams) between different dimensions. Historical sources convey that each shaman forms a bond with a unique animal spirit as a result of their lifelong experiences. During rituals, they ascend to the sky or descend to the underworld depending on the characteristics of this spirit.

"The ceremonial garments and headdresses are produced by the shamans themselves, transforming into tangible carriers of alternative reality layers born from their own life experiences."

This work correlates the experience-based meaning behind historical mask depictions in Central Asia with the behavioral and cognitive layers acquired by the modern individual throughout life. With every situation encountered, the individual develops new attitudes, modes of protection, and reactions. Over time, these accumulations turn into vanishing points positioned around the core self, activated under different conditions.

The overlapping masks in the composition form a historical depiction of this entire pool of experiences. The mask situated in the center points to the state that comes to the forefront in the face of current conditions—representing the state that belongs to "Today".
